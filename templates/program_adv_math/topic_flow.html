🔍 Question Deconstruction

How to read this question:

Before doing any math, train yourself to ask three questions:

  • Action Verbs: What is it asking me to Determine, Calculate, or Prove?
  • How many parts? Does the question say "AND" or "BOTH"? (e.g., "Find the formula AND the sum"). If yes, you must provide TWO final answers!
  • Is there a formula? Can I pull a formula from my sheet immediately based on the keywords?

Interactive Guide: The Art of Deconstruction

Rule #1: Hands off the calculator!

The biggest mistake learners make is rushing into the math. The real challenge of an exam is structurally decoding what the examiner wants before you calculate anything.

Action Verbs

Highlight words like Determine or Prove.

"Determine" means calculate a final value. "Prove" means the logical steps are what get marked, not just the final line!

The "AND" Trap

"Calculate the nth term AND the sum."

If you see an 'AND', write down TWO blank spaces immediately on your paper so you don't forget the first part once you finish the complex calculation.

The Formula Hunt

See Arithmetic Series? Instantly write down \( S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n-1)d] \).

Write formulas in the margin before you even finish reading the question. Your brain will automatically start mapping the given numbers into it.

Mastering the question is half the battle.
