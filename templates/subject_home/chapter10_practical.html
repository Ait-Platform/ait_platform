Objective

Learn how to analyse situations, evaluate options, identify consequences and make sound decisions using evidence and reasoning.

Teacher Preparation

Gather everyday objects and prepare simple real-life scenarios.
Examples: Cup, Bottle, Book, Umbrella, Torch, School bag, Raincoat.

Activity 1 – Choosing the Best Tool

Instruction

Place several objects on a table. Teacher presents a problem. Examples:

  • • You need to carry water.
  • • You need to read in the dark.
  • • It is raining outside.

Teacher asks:

  • • Which object would help most?
  • • Why?

Expected Answer

Learner identifies the object that best solves the problem and explains the reason.

Activity 2 – Identifying Consequences

Instruction

Teacher presents situations. Examples:

  • • A learner forgets to study.
  • • A plant is never watered.
  • • A bicycle is left outside in the rain.

Teacher asks:

  • • What might happen next?

Expected Answer

Learner explains realistic outcomes based on the situation.

Activity 3 – Comparing Solutions

Instruction

Teacher presents a problem.

Example: You need to carry ten books.

Options:

  • • Plastic bag
  • • School bag
  • • Small box

Teacher asks:

  • • Which option is best?
  • • Why?

Expected Answer

Learner selects the most practical solution and justifies the choice.

Activity 4 – Spotting Problems

Instruction

Teacher shows an everyday situation. Examples:

  • • Water leaking from a tap.
  • • Toys blocking a doorway.
  • • Food left uncovered.

Teacher asks:

  • • What is the problem?
  • • Why is it a problem?

Expected Answer

Learner explains the issue and its possible impact.

Activity 5 – Making Decisions

Instruction

Teacher presents a choice.

Example: You have enough money to buy:

  • • One large item
  • or
  • • Three smaller useful items

Teacher asks:

  • • Which would you choose?
  • • Why?

Expected Answer

Learner provides logical reasoning supporting the choice.

Activity 6 – Evidence Investigation

Instruction

Teacher presents clues. Example:

  • • The ground is wet.
  • • Dark clouds are visible.
  • • People are carrying umbrellas.

Teacher asks:

  • • What probably happened?

Expected Answer

Learner concludes that it probably rained.

Activity 7 – Planning Ahead

Instruction

Teacher presents a future situation. Examples:

  • • Going on a picnic.
  • • Travelling on a long trip.
  • • Preparing for a school day.

Teacher asks:

  • • What should you take with you?
  • • Why?

Expected Answer

Learner identifies useful items and explains their importance.

Activity 8 – Best Route Challenge

Instruction

Create two routes to reach an object.

Route A:

  • • Shorter
  • • Contains obstacles

Route B:

  • • Longer
  • • Clear path

Teacher asks:

  • • Which route would you choose?
  • • Why?

Expected Answer

Learner considers safety, speed and practicality before deciding.

Activity 9 – Solving a Real Problem

Instruction

Teacher presents a household or school problem. Examples:

  • • Lost classroom supplies.
  • • Water spilled on the floor.
  • • Books falling from a shelf.

Teacher asks:

  • • What should be done first?
  • • What would solve the problem?

Expected Answer

Learner identifies logical actions to resolve the situation.

Activity 10 – Critical Thinking Investigation

Instruction

Teacher presents a complete scenario. Example:

A learner must walk home.

  • • Route A is short but busy.
  • • Route B is longer but safer.
  • • It is getting dark.

Teacher asks:

  • • What should the learner do?
  • • Why?

Expected Answer

Learner considers safety, risk and consequences before making a recommendation.
