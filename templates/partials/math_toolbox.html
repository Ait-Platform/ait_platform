Mathematical Definitions

Sequence A list of numbers or objects in a special order.
Plain English: A pattern of numbers lined up in a row.
Geometric A sequence made by multiplying by the same value each time (e.g., 2, 4, 8, 16).
Plain English: A pattern where you multiply the previous number by the exact same amount to get the next one.
Arithmetic A sequence made by adding the same value each time (e.g., 1, 4, 7, 10).
Plain English: A pattern where you add or subtract the exact same amount to get to the next number.

Official formulas with usage instructions
Quadratic Formula \( x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \)
Where to use it

When you need to find the roots (x-intercepts) of a quadratic equation (\(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\)) that cannot be easily factorized, or when the question asks for answers "correct to two decimal places".

How to use it

Ensure the equation is in standard form and equal to zero. Identify the coefficients a, b, and c. Substitute them directly into the formula. Be extremely careful when squaring negative b values inside the square root.

Arithmetic Sequences & Series
\( T_n = a + (n-1)d \) \( S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n-1)d] \)
Where to use it

When dealing with a linear pattern where you add or subtract the exact same constant difference (d) to get to the next term.

How to use it

Use \( T_n \) to find the value of a specific term or to solve for the number of terms (\( n \)). Use \( S_n \) to calculate the total sum of the first \( n \) terms. You must identify a (the first term) and calculate d (\( T_2 - T_1 \)) first.

Geometric Sequences & Series
\( T_n = ar^{n-1} \)
\( S_n = \frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1} \text{ for } r \neq 1 \) \( S_{\infty} = \frac{a}{1 - r} \text{ for } -1 < r < 1 \)
Where to use it

When dealing with an exponential pattern where you multiply by the exact same constant ratio (r) to get to the next term.

How to use it

Use \( T_n \) for specific terms. Use \( S_n \) for the sum of a finite sequence. Use \( S_{\infty} \) ONLY when the question asks for the "sum to infinity" and explicitly states or implies the series converges (meaning the ratio r is a fraction between -1 and 1).

⚖️ Mathematical Rules

Exponent Laws 
1. \( x^a \cdot x^b = x^{a+b} \)
2. \( \frac{x^a}{x^b} = x^{a-b} \)
3. \( (x^a)^b = x^{a \cdot b} \)
Plain English: When multiplying bases, add exponents. When dividing, subtract. Power to a power, multiply!

Arithmetic Sequences & Series
  1. Identify the known variables: first term a, common difference d.
  2. If calculating a specific term, write down \( T_n = a + (n-1)d \).
  3. If you need to find n first, substitute knowns into the \( T_n \) formula and solve for n.
  4. If calculating a sum, choose the right formula: \( S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n-1)d] \) OR \( S_n = \frac{n}{2}(a+L) \).
  5. Substitute your variables and solve carefully.
{% elif sub_topic == 'sequences_geometric' %}
Geometric Sequences & Series
  1. Identify the known variables: first term a, constant ratio r.
  2. Find r by dividing any term by its preceding term (\( T_2 / T_1 \)).
  3. Write down the relevant formula: \( T_n = ar^{n-1} \) or \( S_n = \frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1} \).
  4. Substitute variables. If solving for an exponent n, use logarithms!
  5. Calculate final answer.
